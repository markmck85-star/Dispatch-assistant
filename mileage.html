Mileage Check

A quick look at each technician’s biweekly mileage log — not an audit. It flags numbers that cannot be a real drive (a dropped digit, a backward odometer, 200 miles between two stores next door).

What you are looking at

Each hop on the Mileage sheet is compared to the known driving distance between those two stops. Lunch detours and a mid-day bounce home stay quiet. A −2 mile leg or a 3× claim lights up. Techs only write the city for home (e.g. Johns Creek), not the street.

  • looks fine — the miles they wrote line up with the usual drive between those two stops. No need to dig in.
  • flagged — one hop cannot be a real drive. Read that line.
  • Yellow — a stop name we have not learned yet. Not an overcharge; we add an alias and it sticks.
